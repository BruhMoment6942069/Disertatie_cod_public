> 🔧 **MICROSTEPPING IS NOW 1/16** — `STEPS_PER_REV` 200 → 3200. Not a comfort change — a **resolution requirement**. At 1.8 deg/full-step the vergence-angle quantisation alone would be roughly 20% of the distance measurement (≈±40 cm at 2 m); at 1/16 it is ~1.3% (≈±3 cm). Step-unit constants were scaled with it: `ENDSTOP_CLEARANCE` 10 → 160 (a 1.125 deg backoff would not release the endstop, and `stopAndSetHome` would then set zero while still on the switch — nothing re-checks the endstop after homing), `DEADBAND` 3 → 48 (note it is stored as `int8_t`, so 127 is the hard ceiling). Degree-unit constants were correctly left alone; `RANGE_STEPS` recompute themselves. **Rule: anything in steps scales with microstepping, anything in degrees does not.**

> 🌡️ **WARM-LIGHT DETECTION — WALL HIT, EVIDENCE GATHERED (2026-08-06, Machine B). READ BEFORE TOUCHING THE TRACKER.**
> - **Symptom:** under tungsten/warm-bulb light the mask floods — ball, cream cabinet, wall, skin and guitar bodies all land in the same warm hue band. Detection "works somewhat but not good enough."
> - **Physics (settled, do not re-litigate):** a tungsten bulb barely emits blue-green. A yellow-green ball can only reflect wavelengths present in the light, so its hue collapses toward the background's. **No exposure or white-balance setting fixes this** — already killed with data on 07-13 (WB parachute experiment: hue band moved 8-24 vs 7-23, i.e. unchanged).
> - **THE SHARP INSIGHT (new 08-06):** OpenCV computes hue as `60*(G-B)/(R-B)` — **both terms depend on B, the channel tungsten starves and the sensor fills with noise.** So H is a ratio of two noisy near-zero quantities. That is *why* H collapses, not merely that "the ball looks cream." R and G are BOTH well-exposed under tungsten, so **any discriminator built only on R and G stands on solid ground** — notably `G-R` and Lab `a*` (the green↔red axis). Lab `b*` is the dead channel here (everything pins toward yellow); ignore it.
> - **TRIED AND REVERTED — DO NOT REBUILD WITHOUT NEW REASON:** appearance tracking (`cv::TrackerMIL`, seeded from the Hough auto-calib lock, then supervised by Hough with stability + size gates). MIL *did* follow the ball under warm light (proving the scene is beatable), but **drifted onto fingers/wall and never doubts itself**, and **Hough proved an unreliable judge** in this cluttered scene (reacquire points scattered across the frame — it finds "circles" in guitar bodies, cabinet edges, knuckles). Reverted cleanly; `VisionWorker.{cpp,h}` on `v3-main` are byte-identical to `15dca58`. **Note contrib is NOT installed** — only core trackers (MIL/GOTURN/Nano/ViT) exist; CSRT/KCF would need an OpenCV-contrib build.
> - **CHEAPEST REAL FIX, said plainly: a 5000–6500K daylight LED bulb (~$8).** It puts the missing photons back in the room and the EXISTING committed code works with zero changes. Controlling demo lighting is standard practice, not cheating. **This is the recommendation for presentation day.**

Three detector families (colour, shape, appearance) failing for ONE reason (missing signal) is a strong limitations section.

## 📡 Wire protocol (LAW — both sides must match)

UDP port 1234 both ways. All multi-byte ints **little-endian** `int32`.

| Direction | Message | Bytes | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| PC→ESP | handshake | `"Connected"` (9 ASCII) | ESP acks with same string; PC rediscovers via subnet broadcast when `_espAddress` unknown |
| PC→ESP | keep-alive | `0x00` (1 byte) | falls through ESP switch harmlessly → **no message ID may ever be 0** |
| PC→ESP | `MSG_TARGET=2` | `[2][pan i32][tilt i32]` = 9 bytes | **values are DEGREES**; ESP reads at `buf+1`, `buf+5` |
| PC→ESP | `MSG_MODE=3` | `[3][mode u8]` = 2 bytes | mode: `MODE_TRACK=0`, `MODE_TEST=1` |
| ESP→PC | `MSG_MOTOR_POS=1` | `[1][count u8][pos i32 ×count][homed u8]` | streamed at 50 Hz after handshake; self-describing (scales to 4 motors) |

ESP constants live in `ProiectDisertatie/include/config.h` (~lines 39–47). Qt mirrors them as **two public `enum class : quint8` in `UdpClient.h`** (lines 22–23): `MsgID {MOTOR_POS=1, TARGET=2, MODE=3}` and `ModeID {TRACK=0, TEST=1}`. Values are explicit on purpose — they're a wire contract, never let them default.

## 🧠 Decisions made & WHY (do not re-litigate)

### ESP side (from the 07-03 session)
1. **Degrees on the wire, steps only inside the ESP.** Conversion via `stepsPerDegree` (`config.h:41`, full-step 200/rev ⇒ ≈0.556 steps/deg). Clamping happens in `positionControl` *after* conversion — `netTask` must NOT clamp.
2. **Proportional control, not bang-bang, not PID.** Stopping distance at speed 400/accel 1000 is 80 steps > pan's whole 33-step range ⇒ bang-bang physically impossible. `Kp=3`, `deadband=3`, per-motor fields.
3. **NO `cfg.dir` in `positionControl`** — direction comes from sign of error alone; `dir` is homing-only. Injecting it causes positive-feedback runaway on dir-flipped motors.
4. **Limit-trick rejected** (planting a soft limit at target for exact stops) — clobbers calibration limits, fragile restore. P-loop keeps limits armed as backstop.
5. **`volatile uint8_t g_mode` lives in main.cpp** (not config.h — no `inline` vars, toolchain may be pre-C++17). Mode-follow block in `motorRun` re-routes only **between** `T_MOVEMENT`⇄`TEST_MODE`; can never yank a motor out of homing.
6. **Homing barrier:** `homedCount++` fires at backoff completion (H_BACKOFF→H_DONE), NOT at endstop touch — touch-counting had a race.
7. **Panel = mode.** Slider panel visible ⇒ `MODE_TEST`; hidden ⇒ `MODE_TRACK`. (Implementation changed on the Qt side — see below — but the principle stands.)

### Qt side (decided/built 07-06)
8. **Protocol constants are two separate `enum class`es** (message IDs vs mode values — different vocabularies; the near-miss family `MODE_TEST` vs `TEST_MODE` already caused one ESP bug). `enum class` chosen over plain enum deliberately: mixing the vocabularies is now a compile error. Byte packing uses `static_cast<char>` (write side); receive comparisons cast **both** sides to `quint8` (signedness trap — `char` is signed here).
9. **`UdpClient` is the only class that speaks bytes.** Callers speak intent: `sendTarget(qint32 pan, qint32 tilt)`, `sendMode(ModeID)`. Message IDs are hardcoded inside the methods — callers never pass them. `sendMode` takes `ModeID` (not `quint8`) so invalid modes don't compile.
10. **SliderPanel is EMBEDDED in the main window, not a dialog.** User requested toggle-in-window instead of floating dialog. `SliderPanel` now inherits **QWidget** (was QDialog); it sits in `MainWindow.ui` as a **promoted widget** (`class="SliderPanel"` name=`sliderDataWidget`, promotion registered in `<customwidgets>`, mirroring how `graphData` was done). The class builds its own sliders in its constructor — the .ui cell is an empty placeholder (a Designer-children-inside-promoted-widget hybrid was rejected: uic creates children AFTER the constructor runs, so the constructor can't connect to them).
11. **Consequence: the `QDialog::finished` plan is DEAD.** Both mode transitions happen in `MainWindow::handleTestSliders()` (menu-action toggle, `_sliderWindowOpen` bool): show ⇒ emit toggled(true), hide ⇒ emit toggled(false). Visibility and mode can't disagree.
12. **UI layer is decoupled from the network layer — compiler-enforced.** MainWindow does NOT include UdpClient.h. Mode crosses UI→controller as `sliderPannelToggled(bool visible)` (bool = layer-neutral); the **controller** translates bool→`ModeID` (`handleSliderPannelToggled`). User explicitly rejected putting `UdpClient::ModeID` in a MainWindow signal (Option B) and a private-enum variant (private types can't appear in signal signatures — receiver must name them). Each layer speaks its own language; translation at boundaries: bool → ModeID → byte → ESP enum.

- **Clips live in `Qt-WindowsApp/clips/` since the 07-16 relocation** — `cmake-build-debug/clips/` is the DEAD pre-relocation folder and still holds one stale 07-15 file. "Recording is broken" on 07-22 was just looking in the old place; `setRecording` qDebugs the full path on every start, so read the log before hunting folders.
- `HoughCircles` parameter lore banked: `minDist = gray.rows` ⇒ at most one circle reported ⇒ strongest-only for free, no sorting. `param2` is the accumulator threshold and the ONLY knob worth turning (lower = more, weaker circles). `GaussianBlur(9,9),σ2` is mandatory, not optional polish — Hough is noise-paranoid. Sample at **r × 0.8**: the rim carries background bleed, antialiased boundary, and the shadow terminator; sample the belly.

### Added 2026-07-23 (D3 detection + D4 start, Machine B)
- **Fill-ratio beats perimeter-circularity for fuzzy objects.** `4π·area/perim²` is hypersensitive to a ragged/furry boundary — `arcLength` traces every jag, perimeter is squared, the score craters (a round tennis ball read ~0.5). `area/(π·r_enclosing²)` uses no perimeter → immune to edge fuzz (ball ~0.7). Reach for fill-ratio whenever the silhouette is textured; save circularity for clean-edged shapes.
- **Warm/incandescent light casts the WHOLE scene toward one hue** → the color mask lights up everything (ceiling/walls/skin/ball all white). Not a calibration bug; it is *why* the design needs daylight + shape gates. That mask screenshot is a thesis figure. Third proof this project that **color is fragile** (warm hue-collision 07-22, warm color-collapse 07-23, bright-light saturation-clip 07-23) and **shape is the robust fallback**.
- **Auto-calib (Hough) is a controlled-LIVE-moment tool, useless on clips** — cluttered backgrounds + off-size balls spam false circles. Calibrate clips by CLICKING (`sampleAt` works on `_lastRaw`, any source; `clipView` is wired). One calibration per lighting condition.
- **Two Hough knobs, two jobs:** `param2` = confidence (LOOSE to catch a moderate-contrast ball), `maxRadius` = size gate (TIGHT to reject bigger round things like a forehead). Don't fix a *size* problem with *confidence* — raising param2 to reject the forehead strangled the ball too.
- **Clipping ≠ dim.** Overexposure destroys saturation (information lost); CLAHE/histogram equalization redistributes contrast but cannot restore what was clipped. Fix the light/exposure, not the pixels.
- **Cropping shifts coordinates:** a detection inside a sub-`Rect` is in crop coords — add `roi.x/roi.y` to return to frame coords. The shift-only case of the click-mapping sandwich; forget it and every ROI hit lands in the wrong place.

### Added 2026-08-04 (D4 Stage 3 ROI + Stage 4 smoothing, Machine B)
- **The ROI double-offset bug (Stage 3):** `detectBall` cropped `frame(roi)` into `crop` but then built the mask on `frame` (full frame) — so `bestCenter` came back in full-frame coords, and adding `roi.x/roi.y` on top double-counted. ### Added 2026-08-06 (Engage button + warm-light detection dead-end, Machine B)
- **Clone-and-forget in `connect`:** copying a connect line and changing only the SOURCE leaves the DESTINATION pointing at the old slot. Here `actionEngage_Tracking`'s `toggled` was wired to `autoCalibrateToggled` — so "Engage Tracking" would have restarted auto-calibration instead of arming tracking. Caught in review before commit. When cloning a connect, check BOTH ends.
- **Silence does not stop a velocity system.** `setSpeed(v)` is a STANDING ORDER, not a nudge: if the PC just stops sending, the ESP keeps the last speed forever. So Disengage must ACTIVELY send `sendSpeed(0,0)` before flipping `_currentMode` — flipping first means the gate swallows the stop and the motor runs on. ### Added 2026-08-08 (D5 closed loop + two-gimbal scaling, Machine B)
- **A velocity command is a STANDING ORDER, and silence does not cancel it.** The PC disengage sends `sendSpeed(0,0)` before shutting its mode gate, but that only covers the graceful case. Kill the app, drop WiFi or hit a breakpoint and the ESP would hold the last speed forever — hence the `SPEED_TIMEOUT_MS` watchdog in `T_MOVEMENT`: no packet for 300 ms means the command is treated as zero. Any future command that carries a rate needs the same thinking.
- **A `g_`-prefixed name is not a promise the compiler keeps.** First `MSG_SPEED` attempt declared `volatile int32_t g_speed[2]` **inside the case block** — a local, destroyed when the block exited, invisible to the stepper task on the other core. Compiled clean, looked right, did nothing. Check scope, not naming convention.
- **Instrumentation lied three separate times in one session — this is the dominant bug species in this project now.** (1) The ROI rectangle draw sat after a `break` inside `default`, so it had never once executed. (2) The mask debug view called `buildMask` on the FULL frame while `detectBall` only searched the ROI, so harmless background specks looked like threats. (3) `handleBallTracked` logged deg/s *after* calling `sendSpeed`, which silently returns when `_espAddress` is null — so the log showed a perfect stream of commands while nothing left the PC. That last one cost an evening. **Before trusting a readout, confirm it observes the same data path the code actually takes.**
- **Fix orientation in exactly one place.** The frame flip runs before anything else consumes the frame, so mask, click sampling, overlay and clips agree. It also sets the sign of errX/errY, and therefore whether the loop is negative feedback (converges) or positive feedback (drives to the end stop). If you flip the frame AND negate GAIN, they cancel and you get a runaway with no obvious cause.
- **Do not trust a length that came off the wire.** The `MSG_SPEED` parser reads the count byte, clamps it to `MOTOR_COUNT`, and only then checks that `n` is large enough for the claimed payload. Without the clamp a malformed or newer sender writes past `g_speed`; without the length check you parse bytes that never arrived.
- **Soft limits are real but they are not a stop button.** SpeedStepper does decelerate at `setPlusLimit`/`setMinusLimit` (user was right to push back on the "runs into the wall" framing), so a runaway parks at end-of-travel rather than crashing. But it still *goes there* — which is why disengage sends an explicit zero, and why the limits' meaning depends entirely on a good homing zero.

### Added 2026-08-10 (two-gimbal PC scaling: B1–B3 + addressing, Machine B)
- **The "signature lies" family struck twice more, both in one hour.** `setupVisionWorker(int id)` created its worker with `new VisionWorker(0)` — parameter accepted, then ignored, which would have given BOTH workers `_camId = 0` (shared ini, and gimbal 1 never receiving a speed command, since both would emit `ballTracked(0, …)`). And `stopCapture`'s guard read `if (_camId != _camId)` — a member compared to **itself**, always false, so the guard silently never fires. **Whenever you write a comparison or a constructor argument, read it aloud naming both sides**; these compile clean and behave plausibly.
- **`int → const double&` is an implicit conversion, so Qt will happily mis-wire a signal to a stale slot.** `fpsUpdated(int id, double fps)` → `handleFPSUpdate(const double& fps)` compiles without a warning and passes the **id** where the **fps** belongs (HUD reads 0). The "fewer params compiles fine" gotcha is worse than it looks: it is not just truncation, it is *positional re-binding through implicit conversions*. Change signal and slot in the same pass, and pick an acceptance tell that would expose the lie (here: the HUD must read ~30, not 0).
- **`QString::arg` fills the lowest-numbered marker PRESENT, not `%1` specifically.** A typo'd `"rec_cam&1_%2.avi"` meant `.arg(_camId)` filled `%2`, then `.arg(stamp)` found no marker left, warned, and did nothing — producing a **constant filename that overwrote the previous recording every time**. A format-string typo became silent data loss.
- **Uninitialised loop counter:** `for (int i; i < 2; ++i)` in the destructor — UB, may skip the loop or index wild. Trivially fixed, easy to skim past.
- **One writer per slot** (shared-array design rule): when several producers share one output array, each must write only the slots it owns, and only a genuinely global command may write all of them. Gimbal 0 zeroing all four speeds on a one-frame ball loss would have stopped a healthy gimbal 1. Ask of every write: *is this event about me, or about the system?*
- **Addressing pattern for one-signal-many-workers** (chosen over `QMetaObject::invokeMethod`, which is string-based and loses compile-time checking): put the target id in the message and guard at the top of the slot (`if (camId != _camId) return;`). Outbound signals carry the id as a return address and the controller routes on it. Mail in gets checked against your own address; mail out gets a return address.

### Added 2026-08-09 (two-gimbal PC scaling, Machine B)
- **A guard that compares a member to itself always passes.** `stopCapture` read `if (_camId != _camId)`, so the address check never fired and selecting one camera stopped the other's capture. Compiles clean, looks plausible at a glance. When a guard seems not to be working, read it character by character before suspecting the caller.

### Added 2026-08-18/19 (triangulation brought up and calibrated, Machine B)

- **A dead sensor produced measurements that were stable, repeatable, and completely false — and the stability WAS the symptom.** The `MOTOR_POS` stream stopped while vision kept running. Because the speed loop reads `errX` and never touches `_panSteps`, both heads went on tracking the ball perfectly while every fix was solved from step counts 120 steps out of date. Vergence stayed plausible, both heads agreed on height to 4 mm, and the readout sat rock-steady at 1033 mm **at every distance**, because its dominant input had stopped being a variable. Cost an evening. Fixed with `POS_STALE_MS` and a suppression guard. **The rule now applied everywhere: absence of an input must BLANK the readout, never produce a confident one.** Corollary: when a number stops moving, ask whether its inputs are still moving before congratulating yourself on the noise floor.
- **The in-frame X correction was missing its minus, and that single sign defeated three separate calibration attempts.** The function is `inFrameX` in `Triangulator.cpp`. `VisionWorker` mirrors the frame before anything measures `errX`, so a positive `errX` puts the ball to the head's LEFT — negative rotation. Why it hid: with the ball centred the term is a fraction of a degree and enters both bearings alike, so it nearly cancels out of the difference. It stops cancelling the moment the two heads sit at different offsets, which is ALWAYS, because the deadzone lets each stop independently. Wrong-signed, it turned 1.5 deg of slack into 3 deg of vergence error that changed with every ball position — **not noise, but scattering exactly like noise.** Proof procedure, if ever doubted again: hold the ball still, let one head settle, nudge ONLY the other, and check that `psi + inFrameX` comes out unchanged.
- **Two points can always be satisfied exactly, and therefore can never be checked.** Three separate two-point fits of `zeroSteps` were made and all three were wrong — each held at the points fitted and broke at the next distance. Four points against two unknowns is what finally distinguished a fit from a coincidence. **Over-determination here is not rigour for its own sake; it is the only available error signal.**
- **READ THE SHAPE OF THE ERROR, NOT ITS SIZE.** Plot error against range before fitting anything. A percentage that GROWS with range is a differential zero error (fixed angle divided by a vergence that collapses as 1/d). A FLAT percentage is a baseline scale error. A percentage that JUMPS AROUND means something upstream is broken and fitting will only chase it — that third case is what cost the day above.
- **Common-mode and differential errors have different causes and are invisible to different measurements.** Both heads share `M*_PARK_DEG`, so an error in that one constant lands identically on both — 12.6 deg of common-mode. Each head homes on its OWN endstop, and two switches on printed brackets do not trip at the same angle — 2.85 deg of differential. **Range sees only the difference; lateral position sees only the sum.** One number could never fix both, which is why the fit kept failing. Two faults, two causes, two measurements.
- **A sign error hides as an enormous offset in the constant that follows it.** `tiltSign` was `+1` and simply backwards. The tell was not the sign itself but the absurdity downstream: with `+1` the tilt zeros had to sit 96 and 157 steps from park to explain a ball at plate height; with `-1` they land at 8 and 25, which is what "parks roughly level" is supposed to mean. **If a fitted constant comes out implausibly large, suspect a sign upstream before accepting the number.**
- **The blob-radius ratio is a free, independent check that a lock is real.** Radius goes as 1/distance, so `r0/r1` must equal `d1/d0`. Worth having because I got this wrong out loud: from stable blob radii I concluded both cameras were tracking background clutter and reverted a good calibration, when a screenshot showed both cleanly circling the ball. The ratio test settled it in one line (22.7/21.6 = 1.05 against 1295/1203 = 1.08). **Check the cheap invariant before reverting work.**
- **Horizontal range and slant range are not interchangeable, and the UI was reading the wrong one.** `distance` is `hypot(x,y)`, the footprint on the plate; `slant` is the 3D radius from the dot, and slant is what a tape stretched from the centre hole reads. The rig's deliverable is a radius from a point, not a projection onto the floor. At plate height they agree to sub-millimetre, which is exactly why the bug survived — 300 mm of ball height at 1 m is 45 mm of difference, and that reads like a systematic bias.
- **Camera height must be measured to the PLATE, not the floor.** The constant is `camHeight`. It is the datum for `z`, and `slant` combines `z` with `x`/`y` taken from the pan-axis midpoint — which IS the dot. Measure to the floor and slant silently mixes two datums. It also has to be right BEFORE fitting the baseline: a wrong value is a close-range bias that shrinks with distance, and the near points are what pin the slope down.
- **A dropped packet must be noisier than a delivered one.** A short `MOTOR_POS` fell off the end of the parse chain with no emit, no log, no counter — which is what let the controller hold stale positions forever. Silence on a failure path is how a fault becomes an evening.
- **Edge-trigger a suppression guard in BOTH directions, with ONE flag.** First attempt at the staleness guard declared `complained` and `wasStale` separately and never set the second, making the recovery message unreachable. A stream that dies and comes back must announce the recovery as loudly as the failure, or it looks like it never died. The recovery branch is only reachable if the same variable that suppressed the spam is the one that clears it.
- **A round-trip test cannot verify a convention it shares.** The Triangulator suite cannot detect a flipped `inFrameY` sign, because `project()` uses the same convention and the error cancels. The suite passes either way. Documented in the source so nobody trusts it for that. **Ask of every test: what class of bug is it structurally blind to?**
- **Focal length can be measured with no test chart at all.** Park the ball, nudge ONE head a known number of steps, read how far `errX` moved on that camera. 4 steps is 0.45 deg and moved `errX` by 0.0202, which gives 823. The ball-radius route agreed independently at ~780. Only `frameWidth/fx` matters as a RATIO — `errX` arrives already normalised, so an inaccurate `frameWidth` is absorbed. Do not "fix" one without refitting the other.
- **CAD is right about offsets and wrong about scale factors.** `dLat` and `camHeight` came straight from CAD and are fine, because print shrinkage is fractional and 0.5% of 124 mm is sub-millimetre. `baseline` is 500 mm of printed plate, so the same 0.5% is 2.5 mm of pure scale error multiplying EVERY distance the rig will ever report. It fitted to 496.5. **Offsets tolerate a fractional error; scale factors do not.**
- **Exiting the park state on position alone made the park position unrepeatable.** `positionControl` commands `speed = Kp * error`, needing about 9x the error in deceleration — ~900 steps/s² at error 100, against a configured 500 — so the axis reaches the deadband still moving and coasts tens of steps past target. Pan settled at 755 steps one run and 694 the next. Adding a `getSpeed()` check makes every run park at (694, 240, 694, 240), and **that repeatability is the entire foundation of the geometric calibration.**
